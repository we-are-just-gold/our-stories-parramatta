If people ask

The answers you will need

Do I have to stay all day? No. People can come to one session, two or all three.

Do I have to speak? No. Coming just to listen is taking part. Nobody is asked to share anything they do not want to, and people can use their mother tongue.

When can I come in? The welcome desk opens 30 minutes before each session.

Is there lunch? Yes, at 12pm for everyone attending any session. All food is halal, with gluten free, vegetarian and vegan options.

People with a nut allergy or any other allergy are asked to tell the welcome desk.

Photos, filming and consent

Your image, your choice

We are taking photos and video. People are in unless they say no, and saying no is easy.

  • A bright sticker means: do not photograph or film me, wide shots included. Stickers are at the welcome desk.
  • Camera free tables stay out of frame. Facilitators will point them out.
  • Nobody under 18 joins any session until a parent or guardian has filled in the consent form for them.
  • Signed on the day? Ask to see the “Thank you. That is in.” screen on their phone before they go in. For anyone under 18, see it on the parent’s or guardian’s phone.
  • No phone? They can fill in the consent form on the welcome desk device. Paper copies of the consent form and both surveys are at the welcome desk too.
  • Nobody is named unless they choose to be.
  • People are told to tell any volunteer if they change their mind. If someone tells you, tell Kyriakos so the photo or clip is deleted.

Looking after people

Some stories bring things up

That is normal. A counsellor is on site from about 11am, and there is a support card on every table.

Do

  • Thank every person who speaks, and every person who listens.
  • Keep time gently.
  • Watch for distress: going quiet, shaking, tears, leaving suddenly.
  • Offer a break and the counsellor, quietly.

Do not

  • Dig into painful detail.
  • Ask anyone to repeat or explain their story.
  • Share a circle’s story outside it, or put a name to it.
  • Promise to keep a disclosure of harm secret.

If someone is distressed

Ask them privately what would help. Offer the quiet space and the counsellor. Do not leave them alone if they do not want to be. Point to the support card on the table. Tell Kyriakos or Thom once they are looked after.

If someone discloses harm or is at risk

Listen calmly. Do not investigate and do not promise secrecy. Tell Kyriakos or Thom straight away. For young people, the partner organisation’s child protection procedures apply.

In immediate danger, call triple zero (000).
